## Why Subsurface Drainage Matters

Standing water on the surface is easy to see. Excess water within the soil profile can be just as damaging.

Poorly drained soils can stay saturated longer after snowmelt or heavy rain. That can delay fieldwork, restrict root development, and make it harder to manage planting and harvest schedules.

Research on the Devils Lake Basin also notes that some regional soils have slow intake rates, shallow groundwater influence, and a need for internal drainage.

Well-planned agricultural drainage in Ramsey County, ND can help remove excess water from the root zone more efficiently while giving farmers greater control over field conditions.

## Built Around the Farm, Not a Template

Every field has different elevations, soil types, slopes, and drainage limitations. That is why we do not approach tile work with a standard layout.

We evaluate the field, identify problem areas, and build the system around how the land actually behaves. That may mean pattern tiling across a larger area, targeting specific low spots, or designing mains and laterals around existing field conditions.

Our team also helps with permitting questions. Ramsey County’s Water Resource Board plays an active role in local water management and drainage oversight, so understanding the regulatory side of a project is an important part of planning.
