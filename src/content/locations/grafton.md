## Drainage Matters in the Red River Valley

The Red River Valley was formed from the bed of glacial Lake Agassiz. Its flat landscape and fertile soils created excellent agricultural ground, but that same geography can make natural drainage slow. The region supports crops such as wheat, corn, sugar beets, potatoes, soybeans, and dry beans, making timely field access especially important.

However, there are excess-water problems across the valley and growing interest in subsurface drainage as a way to remove water from the soil profile.

For Walsh County farmers, drainage planning is not simply about one visible wet spot. The goal is to understand how water moves through the entire field and where better subsurface management can make the greatest difference.

## Designed Around the Field

Two farms a few miles apart may require very different drainage layouts.

Soil texture, elevation changes, existing outlets, field shape, and cropping plans all affect system design. We do not install a standard pattern simply because it worked somewhere else.

Instead, we identify where excess water is limiting the field and determine the appropriate spacing, depth, mains, and laterals based on those conditions.

That field-specific approach is particularly important for agricultural drainage in Walsh County, ND, where productive soils and relatively flat terrain make careful grade control essential.

## What Better Water Management Can Support

Subsurface drainage is designed to remove excess water from the soil profile rather than relying solely on surface runoff.

When conditions allow the system to work as designed, better drainage can support:

- Earlier and more predictable field access
- Improved trafficability for equipment
- Less prolonged saturation around crop roots
- More consistent working conditions across the field
- Better use of acres that regularly stay wet

Drain tile does not eliminate weather risk, and every field responds differently. Its value comes from giving excess water a planned path out of the soil instead of leaving drainage entirely to natural conditions.
